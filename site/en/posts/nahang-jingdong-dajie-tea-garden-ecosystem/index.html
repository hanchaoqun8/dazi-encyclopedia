Nahang Tea Garden Ecosystem in Jingdong's Dajie Township

Overview

Nahang (那罕) is a village in Dajie Township, Jingdong County, Yunnan, China. This report documents long-term ecological observations from the tea gardens surrounding Nahang village, focusing on the relationship between terroir, processing methods, and the resulting characteristics of Jingdong Dajie Township Pu-erh tea. The ecological monitoring system implemented in these gardens tracks multiple variables including microclimate data, soil composition indicators, and plant biodiversity metrics. These records are maintained using standardized observation protocols that document picking batch information, daily weather conditions, detailed drying process logs, and periodic storage environment assessments. The Dazhi brand has established these gardens as a reference site for understanding the unique characteristics of tea from this specific geographical location.

History & Origin

The tea cultivation tradition in Nahang village dates back several generations, with ancient tea trees in the area estimated to be between 60-100 years old. These trees grow at elevations ranging from 1,400 to 1,650 meters above sea level, contributing to the distinct profile of Jingdong Dajie Township Pu-erh tea. Historical records maintained by local tea associations indicate that the area has been cultivated for tea since the early 20th century, though systematic documentation of growing conditions only began in the early 2000s. The village name "Nahang" (那罕) is believed to derive from the local language, with "Na" meaning tea and "Hang" referring to the specific geographical formation where the trees grow.

Craft & Processing

Processing methods in Nahang follow traditional techniques adapted to modern standards while preserving essential characteristics. Fresh leaves undergo withering in bamboo trays under controlled temperature conditions, with processors recording the duration and environmental factors during this stage. The fixation process is carefully monitored using traditional wok-firing methods, with operators noting the leaf color changes and aroma development. Rolling is performed using both mechanical and hand-rolling techniques, with documentation of the pressure applied and time elapsed. The drying process is particularly meticulous, with temperature and humidity recorded at regular intervals, followed by storage in a controlled environment where temperature and humidity are monitored periodically.

Appearance, Aroma & Taste

The dry leaves of Nahang tea exhibit a dark brown to black color with occasional golden tips, reflecting the careful processing methods. The wet leaves unfurl to show a mix of mature and younger leaves, with some stems intact. The brewed liquor presents a deep amber to reddish-brown color that intensifies with subsequent infusions. The aroma is characterized by notes of earthiness, subtle floral undertones, and a distinctive mineral quality attributed to the local terroir. The taste profile reveals a smooth, mellow character with hints of dried fruit, developing into a clean finish with lingering sweetness. Daily drinking experiences show consistency across different harvest years, though subtle variations occur based on seasonal weather patterns recorded during the growing period.

How to Brew

Brewing Nahang tea requires attention to water temperature and steeping times to extract its full potential. The recommended water temperature ranges from 90-95°C (194-203°F). For a gaiwan or small teapot, use 7-8 grams of leaf per 100ml of water. The initial infusion should be brief, approximately 10-15 seconds, with subsequent infusions gradually increasing in time. The tea typically yields 6-8 flavorful infusions, with the third to fifth infusions generally considered the peak period for flavor development. Brewing parameters are documented in the garden records and adjusted based on seasonal variations in leaf characteristics.

Benefits & Who It Suits

Daily consumption of Nahang tea offers a sensory experience that appeals to tea enthusiasts seeking traditional Pu-erh characteristics. The tea's smooth profile and complex flavor development make it suitable for both casual drinkers and connoisseurs. Its moderate nature allows for multiple infusions throughout a session, making it ideal for gatherings or solitary enjoyment. The tea's characteristics make it particularly suitable for those who appreciate the evolution of flavor over multiple steepings and the traditional processing methods that define Jingdong Dajie Township Pu-erh tea.

Cautions & Considerations

When purchasing or storing Nahang tea, attention should be paid to the environmental conditions. The tea is sensitive to extreme temperature fluctuations and strong odors. Proper storage in a controlled environment helps maintain its quality over time. Buyers should be aware of variations between different harvest years and processing batches, as recorded in the garden documentation. The tea should be stored away from direct sunlight and in an environment with stable humidity levels to prevent degradation of its characteristic flavors.

Buying, Authentication & Storage

Authentic Nahang tea can be identified through several markers, including the producer's documentation system that tracks each batch from garden to market. Buyers should request information about the specific harvest date and processing methods. The packaging should include batch numbers that can be cross-referenced with the garden records. Storage recommendations include keeping the tea in a dedicated tea storage area with stable temperature and humidity levels. The garden maintains detailed records of storage conditions for their reserve lots, which can serve as a reference for proper aging parameters.

Final Thoughts

The long-term ecological observation system in Nahang provides valuable insights into the relationship between terroir, processing methods, and final product characteristics. These documented records help preserve traditional knowledge while adapting to modern standards. The consistency in quality across different harvest years demonstrates the effectiveness of the monitoring systems in place. For tea enthusiasts, Nahang represents a specific expression of Jingdong Dajie Township Pu-erh tea, with its own distinctive characteristics shaped by the unique growing conditions and processing methods documented in these garden records.
